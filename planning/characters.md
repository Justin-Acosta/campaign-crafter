Hadar, The Dark Hunger:
An ancient Elder Evil from the Far Realm, Hadar manifests not as a creature of flesh, but as a malevolent, dying star. It hangs in the void, a cinder-red point of light that flickers and dances with a sickening rhythm, unlike any other star in the night sky. Driven by a desperate and insatiable hunger to escape its own slow annihilation, Hadar projects its influence across the cosmos, sending its Heralds to Toril to feast on the life force of mortals. It is a being of immense and unknowable power, promising dark gifts to those warlocks who are mad enough to seek its patronage, twisting their bodies and minds to serve its all-consuming hunger.

Heralds of Hadar - The Heralds are not monsters, but ordinary people who have made a desperate pact with an ancient entity in exchange for power. Each was driven to Hadar's service by a profound, personal crisis—a desire to protect a loved one, a thirst for knowledge, or a need to escape a life of powerlessness. While the pact has marked them, twisting their bodies and saddling them with a gnawing hunger, it has not erased their humanity. They still feel empathy and regret, and their actions, however brutal, are often guided by a twisted, but understandable, logic. They are all gifted with Devil's Sight, allowing them to see through magical darkness, as well as the Awakened Mind feature, which grants them the ability to communicate telepathically. Their spell repertoire always includes Arms of Hadar and Hunger of Hadar, tangible manifestations of their patron's power. It is common for these individuals to multiclass, blending Hadar's dark power with other martial or arcane disciplines.

Avon:
Avon is a human man in his late fifties, with a kind, weathered face framed by short, graying hair that catches the light like spun gold. His robes are the color of a sunrise, simple yet immaculate, and he carries himself with the gentle authority of a beloved community leader. He often speaks with his hands clasped before him, only gesturing to emphasize a point of particular importance. His voice is a calm and measured baritone, but a quiet urgency enters his tone when he speaks of the temple, betraying the deep worry beneath his serene exterior.

Elara:
A Selunite wood elf priestess and childhood friend of Avon, Elara is currently missing after an undead infestation overran the Temple of Selune. Her disappearance is what drives Avon to seek the party's help.

Lyra:
Lyra is a silver dragonborn, her scales shimmering like mother-of-pearl under the festival lights. This gives her a striking, almost ethereal presence that seems at odds with the joyous festival atmosphere around her. Her simple, silver robes seem slightly too large for her, as if they belong to someone else. Her face is etched with worry, and though she offers a soft, kind smile to those who approach her, her eyes, the color of a twilight sky, frequently drift towards the shadows of the Kryptgarden Forest. Her voice is soft and melodic, but it carries an undercurrent of sorrow and anxiety. She is deeply concerned for Elara, her friend and mentor, and feels the heavy weight of responsibility now that she must step into her role during the Eclipse Ritual.

Ghaliver Longstocking:
Ghaliver is a gnome with a wild shock of white hair and grease-smudged cheeks, Ghaliver is a well-known and trusted artificer. Their presentation shifts from day to day, sometimes favoring elaborate, tailored coats and at other times simple, practical overalls. Despite their eccentric mannerisms—like constantly fidgeting with a small metallic contraption and speaking in a rapid-fire, enthusiastic tone—the villagers of Westbridge rely on them to solve complex problems and manage the logistics for key goods. Their eyes, magnified by a pair of thick goggles, dart around with restless curiosity. Their workshop, "Ghal's Gadgets," sits in the center of town, but it is closed for the festival. While they are not directly involved in the planning or celebration of the Eclipse Festival, their creations can occasionally be seen delighting travelers at the festival.

The Warlocks:

Gruff - Gruff is a hulking goliath, his powerful frame a shield for the fierce, protective loyalty he holds for Minda. He is clad in heavy, dark plate armor, the metal etched with grotesque designs that seem to writhe, but beneath the monstrous exterior is a simple, desperate goal: to protect the only person who ever mattered to him. He has always been a man of few words, a trait that became a source of anguish when a childhood tragedy stole Minda's voice. His thoughts, now broadcast through their shared psychic bond, are not just rage and hunger, but a constant, roaring fire of devotion and a deep-seated fear of losing her again. He sees the abduction of Avon not as an act of evil, but as a necessary evil—a sacrifice required to maintain the power he needs to keep Minda safe. He carries a greatsword.

Vexia - Vexia is a lithe tiefling with skin the color of a deep twilight violet. She was a gifted, but overlooked, mage's apprentice, constantly dismissed because of her heritage. Burning with ambition and a desperate need to prove her worth, she sought out Hadar, who offered her the power she craved. She carries herself with a serpentine grace, a confident mask for her deep-seated insecurity. She is the reluctant leader of the trio, driven by a desire to finally be seen as powerful and in control. While her methods are ruthless, she often feels a pang of sympathy for her victims, seeing in them the same powerlessness she fought so hard to escape. She carries a dagger and staff.

Nexi - Nexi is a half-elf, a being from the Shadowfell who has felt the soul-crushing despair of that plane. They sought out Hadar not for personal gain, but for the power to feel something—anything—other than the oppressive gloom of their home. The pact, and the hunger it brings, is a constant, sharp sensation that reminds them they are alive. They cling to the shadows, their movements twitchy and alert, not out of malice, but from a lifetime of learned caution. They view the abduction as a grim necessity, a task that must be done to keep the gnawing emptiness at bay.

Minda - Minda is a gaunt drow woman. Her pale face is dominated by the scar tissue where her lower jaw once was, a brutal injury from her youth that left her permanently mute. For years, she could only communicate through frantic gestures. It was this desperate silence that drove Gruff to seek out Hadar's power, a choice she supported. The pact was a twisted answer to their prayers: it took her hands, her only way to speak to the world, but gave her a voice inside Gruff’s mind. She endures the hunger and the whispers of Hadar as a price worth paying for this connection. While she understands the fear she inspires, she sees their actions as the only way to preserve the bond that saved them both from isolation.
